06 · How it works, with no labels

Seeing the shelf without being told what is right

1 Perspective-warp every planogram area to a square crop. These are the real crops of camera 6215.

2 How full? stock

Trays and crates look the same under every product; fruit does not. DINOv2 patches split into surface and product; CLIP names them. Fill = product share. Your brief allowed a fill level: piles defeat counting, fill does not.

3 Right product? compliance

CLIP zero-shot on your catalogue names (MACA GALA KG → gala apple), at base-fruit level: the products were chosen to be recognised generically. Empty or hidden counts as compliant.

4 Your scoring rule is our decision rule

Wrong product costs 2. Too low costs 2, too high costs 3. For every area we pick the answer with the best expected score, so when two stock levels are equally likely, the rule picks the lower one.

5 Boxes and counts for staff

OWLv2, a free open-vocabulary detector, draws the products behind Mostrar deteções and counts them per area: ≈ N visible units, always marked as an estimate.

Camera 6215: Pese aqui, the scale on the gondola.

Your scales can teach our cameras

Your store already weighs every kilo of loose produce it sells. That is free supervision. Pilot design

  • Kilos sold per product per hour vs the drop in shelf fill: calibrate each store, no AI labeller
  • Check sales speed against real sales
  • Fill drops with no sale: culling or shrink, a waste signal for MC’s goal to halve food waste by 2028

And beyond

  • Most modern stores already have ceiling cameras: beyond autonomous stores
  • Other loose products: bakery, flowers
  • Next closing-time mode: after a cut-off, only empty and wrong product, so no over-restocking of perishables

Honest limit: one store, one day of pictures so far.

Our ask: a 4-week shadow pilot. Week 1 silent at Leiria, against your restock logs; weeks 2–4 a second store. We propose to pass it at ≥95% of empty shelves caught and at most 1 wasted walk in 10.
